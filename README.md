Bitcoin bull and bear cycles, 2017 to 2026

The pattern is that each cycle has been shaped by the halving, an overheated peak, a roughly year-long crash, and a slow rebuild. The 2025-26 cycle follows this outline but has been noticeably milder.

Patterns that repeated

1. The peak lands about 12 to 18 months after the halving. All three tops came roughly 17 to 18 months after the halving, and the 2025 top fits that timing. The halving cuts new coin supply, and the standard argument is that this feeds a supply squeeze once demand builds.

2. The bull run is about three years, and the bear about one. One cycle guide puts the historical climb from low to high at around 1,060 days and the fall to the next low at around 370 days. Another analysis found the average peak-to-bottom time was 387 days.

3. Bear markets take a long time to recover. The same analysis found recovering to the previous all-time high averaged about 643 days after the bottom. That was around 730 days for 2013 and 2017, and about 480 for 2021, when ETF-era inflows helped.

4. Retail euphoria came before the top, and despair came before the bottom. In 2017 it was ICOs and mainstream hype, and in 2021 it was NFTs, meme coins and leverage. Bottoms brought "Bitcoin is dead" headlines and extreme fear. A June 2026 report had the Fear & Greed index at 18 with Bitcoin near $59.7K.

5. Altcoins peak late and fall harder. Money tends to rotate from Bitcoin into altcoins near the end of a run. Today's rally already shows early signs of this, with Bitcoin dominance falling since late July.

6. Bear markets contain sharp rallies. After the 2022 lows, Bitcoin rallied roughly 40% in early 2023, which is a reminder that a big bounce doesn't prove a new bull market. Today's rally is a roughly 45% gain from the June low, by my arithmetic.

7. Each bear market has been shallower. One source lists drawdowns of about 94%, 87%, 84% and 77% across successive cycles. Wintermute says the current one sits near 50% and links that to earlier participation from ETFs and institutions.

Each cycle in more detail

2016-2018: retail mania

The halving in July 2016 was followed by a slow grind, then a vertical run through 2017 driven by ICOs and first-time retail buyers.
The peak came in December 2017 near $19.7K. It ended in the classic way, with mainstream media saturation and futures launching on CME and Cboe right around the top,
The bear market lasted about a year, into December 2018, with a drawdown of around 84%. The fall was slow and grinding, with several failed bounces.

2020-2022: liquidity and leverage

The COVID crash of March 2020 was followed by the May halving and enormous monetary stimulus. Companies and institutions began buying, and DeFi, NFTs and meme coins added retail excitement.
The cycle had two peaks, about $64K in April 2021 and about $69K in November 2021. That double top is a reminder that "the peak" is only obvious in hindsight.
The bear market was driven by Fed tightening plus a chain of failures (Terra/Luna, Celsius, Three Arrows, FTX). The low was in November 2022, about 77% below the peak.

2024-2026: the ETF cycle

Spot ETFs launched in January 2024, before the April halving, which is unusual because the big catalyst arrived early. The peak came in October 2025 around $126K.
The bear market has been shallower. Bitcoin fell about 54% to a June 2026 low near $58,000, according to Bitcoin.com, and it has recovered much of that. August 2026 gained 24%, the strongest month since November 2024.
It's unresolved whether June was the final low.


On-chain indicators people watch

Realized price. This is the average price at which all coins last moved, a rough measure of the market's aggregate cost basis. Bitcoin approached realized price this cycle, which sat near $52,750, and prior cycle bottoms have tended to form near or below it.
200-week moving average. A long-run trend line that bitcoin has historically touched near major lows. 21Shares reported that Bitcoin traded right on it for the first time since 2023, and that the level held in both February and June.
Long-term holder behavior. In the same report, 21Shares says long-term holders were adding coins, not selling, with their supply at an all-time high.
50/100-week moving-average convergence. This has happened in 2015, 2019 and 2022, each within weeks of a cycle bottom. 21Shares flags it as a lagging, coincident signal, not a leading one.
Balanced Price. An older-coin spending metric that has flagged deep-cycle bottoms. Analysts say the time spent below it has shrunk from weeks to about a day.

How altcoins behaved

2017-18: Bitcoin's share of the total market fell from very high levels to roughly a third as thousands of ICO tokens launched. ETH peaked around $1,400 in January 2018 and fell about 94% by the end of 2018. Most ICO tokens went to near zero.
2021-22: ETH peaked around $4,870 in November 2021 and fell about 80% to its 2022 low. Riskier assets fell further. Solana, a top performer in 2021, fell over 90% after FTX collapsed.
2025-26: ETH again set a high in 2025 (around $4,900, from memory) and is now around $2,600–$2,800, so it is down heavily from that peak. Bitcoin dominance has been falling since late July, and some analysts see early altcoin-season signals

Across cycles, altcoins rally harder in the euphoric stage and fall harder in the bear market. Many never recover their highs, so an altcoin bounce is a much riskier bet than a Bitcoin bounce
